## Deliberately not included (and why)

- **Exercises, answer keys and the books' own example sentences** (RW Ch 1–2, 4–10, 15–17; BULTO) are not reproduced, for copyright reasons. Every skill they practise is taught with new examples.
- **Pure vocabulary lists** (RW opposites, weather words, celestial words, money and wealth word lists; BULTO themed lists) belong to vocab.json and phrases.json. Only the grammar they show is covered here.
- **Regional forms that are not standard** appear only as variant notes and are not taught as main forms. These include RW's future -uuf jiraadha, the continuous forms -utti jira and -aati jira, the imperative beenu, kaachuu "run", and the eastern feminine possessives tiyya/tee, as well as BULTO's "ala" for "without". BULTO's errors (non-standard jiraachuu negatives, "bitne", "nyaati / nyaana", separated "dha", "kana" as "this" in subject position) are corrected without comment in the lessons.
- **RW's claim that Oromo words never end in a consonant** is corrected (words like bishaan, afaan, isin end in n).
- **Some RW plural examples** (ijjeen "eyes", the wowwoota typo) and the uncertain causative paradigms of s-stem verbs for ati/isheen are left out, to keep every example certain.

**Totals:** 2,679 items in 71 categories. 384 items are flagged `"check": true`.

**New category ids** (none of the canonical ids fit):
- `basics` (group basics): yes/no/please/thank you, greetings and other very short fixed expressions.
- `holidays` (group society): holiday names, celebrations, gifts and holiday greetings.
- `general` (group extras): abstract nouns from the book's NON-CATEGORIZED list that fit no topic, such as *idea, method, process, success*.

**How the counts work.** A count is the number of items kept from that section after de-duplication. De-duplication uses the same English and the same Oromo across the whole file; the first occurrence is kept. When a word was already listed under an earlier section, it is counted there and not again. Sentences were skipped because phrases are handled by another agent. Short fixed expressions of four words or fewer were kept with pos `phrase`. The Oromo was standardized to STYLE.md, and every book form that differs is recorded in the item's note.
